# The teaser's tooling

Everything that makes the game's teaser, kept so it can be made again whenever the game changes. It is its own workspace package; nothing else imports it and it ships nothing to players. The game's colours, face and wordmark letters come from `@japacity/brand` (`packages/brand/`).

## The commands, from the repository's root

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `pnpm teaser:record` | Plays the real game on a development server (it starts one if none answers) and records the moments the cut shows as WebM clips under `apps/teaser/clips/`, through Chromium's screencast; the wide shots hide every surface over the scene so the world stands alone, and the tall ones (`tall-day`, `tall-sofa`, `tall-ride`, `tall-street`, `tall-makoko`, `tall-night`) are the same sessions on the phone at scale 3, cropped to 1080 by 1920 for the portrait teaser. The screencast sends frames at the device's pixels only from a browser started at that scale, so the wide sessions run in a browser started at 1.5 (true 1920 by 1080) and the tall ones in one started at 3, one browser open at a time. Every session starts from the life the front door makes for it: Ada the nurse, and for `wide-makoko` and `tall-makoko` Bisi, the child of the water, whose life starts in Makoko (by morning, where the stilts, the water and the canoes read apart). `--port 5183` picks the server's port and `--only tall-day,tall-night` records only the clips named, leaving every other clip as it is (`--only wide-day,wide-night,wide-sofa,wide-ride,wide-street,wide-makoko` records the landscape's clips again). Needs Chromium for Playwright (`pnpm exec playwright install chromium`) and ffmpeg on the path (or `FFMPEG=/path/to/ffmpeg`) |
| `pnpm --filter @japacity/teaser sound` | Makes the thirteen sounds from code under `apps/teaser/public/sound/` (the chilled bed of pad, thumb piano and shaker, the danfo's horn and engine, the crowd, the pops, the swish, the coins, the stamp, the chime, the night, the jet, the switch and the swell), so they are the makers' own |
| `pnpm teaser:render` | Renders the teaser in its two frames as H.264 with its sound: `Lead` to `docs/teaser/japacity-teaser.mp4` (1920 by 1080) and `LeadPortrait` to `docs/teaser/japacity-teaser-portrait.mp4` (1080 by 1920), and stops if a file reaches 10 MB. `--only LeadPortrait` renders one; `--stills <folder>` renders the named moments of `src/lead/stills.ts` as PNG files instead: the portrait teaser's opening, street, turn, night in the hand and end card, its busiest frame with a phone feed's bars drawn over it (`portrait-feed`), and the landscape's end card. If Remotion's own browser does not open the bundle, `TEASER_BROWSER` names another Chromium headless shell (Playwright's, under `~/Library/Caches/ms-playwright`) |
| `pnpm --filter @japacity/teaser studio` | Opens Remotion's studio to scrub any composition frame by frame |
| `npx remotion still src/index.ts Poster out.png` (from `apps/teaser`) | One frame of the cut with a play button over it, for anywhere a still has to stand for the video; `--props='{"frame":250}'` picks the moment |

## Putting a new render on the front page

GitHub plays a video inline only from an asset uploaded through its own editor, never from a file in the repository (a repository link opens the file's own page, where it has to be downloaded). So after `pnpm teaser:render`, a person signed in to GitHub opens `README.md` in the web editor (or any issue's comment box), drags `docs/teaser/japacity-teaser.mp4` in, and GitHub answers with a line like `https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/<id>`. That line replaces the one under the teaser comment at the top of `README.md`, on a line of its own with a blank line each side. The file in the repository stays the source; the asset is a copy GitHub keeps. Drag `docs/teaser/japacity-teaser-portrait.mp4` in too, for a second asset line. Under the player stand two pictures at one height, each a link to its uploaded asset (the landscape's and the portrait's), so a tap plays it in the browser, also where GitHub's app shows no player, with the caption linking the files in `docs/teaser/`: the landscape's end card and the portrait's opening, `docs/teaser/japacity-teaser-end.webp` and `docs/teaser/japacity-teaser-portrait-open.webp`, made from the `landscape-end` and `portrait-open` stills of `pnpm teaser:render --stills <folder>`, resized to 600 pixels high and saved as WebP (`cwebp -q 82 -resize 0 600`).

## The compositions, in `src/`

- `lead/Lead.tsx`: the teaser as shipped, in two frames from one list of shots: `Lead` (1920 by 1080) and `LeadPortrait` (1080 by 1920, for a phone's feed). The world's recordings with the game's pieces inside them: a word bubble in each place's voice, the band whose age carries the months, Makoko on the lagoon before the house (Bisi's band and the fish seller's call), the brief's chip, the passbook over the house, the four ways out (a master's in the UK, a tech job abroad, health care in the UK, the Gulf), the night falling, the flight with the lights joke, the seal's bulbs clicking off and on; and the sounds laid on each shot. The portrait teaser is the game in the hand from the first shot: it opens on the same card stacked, then every shot of the world is the phone's own recording, full frame, with the pieces where the game puts them on a phone and each in its zone of a feed's frame (`parts/zones.tsx`): the band at 220 to 320, the bubbles over heads in the picture, the beat's words as one centred chip at 1180 to 1360, the cards at 1000 to 1540 (the four ways out at 120 pixels high, from 1006 to 1534), and nothing of ours above 220 or below 1540, where a feed draws its own bars. Both end cards close on GitHub's mark and "Free. Open source on GitHub."
- `lead/Poster.tsx`: a frame of the cut with a play button, a still to stand for the video.
- `teaser/`: the thirty second `Teaser` composition (the title cards, the map push, the night fall, the flight, the end card); its scenes `Flight`, `MapShot` and `NightFalls` are the ones `lead/Lead.tsx` uses.
- `deck/`: the `Deck` composition, a deck of cards beside a caption with a window that grows into the world, open in the studio.
- `parts/`: the dress at a trailer's scale (the card, the chip, the sticker, the pressed button, the pill, the title, the phone, the plane), the seal, the wordmark and GitHub's mark (`GitHub_Invertocat_White.svg`, kept as GitHub hands it out at https://github.com/logos, drawn from the same path with only its fill set), the motion springs, the frame a cut is drawn in (`frame.ts`, wide or tall, with the two compositions' sizes), which the pieces read to lay themselves out, and the feed's zones (`zones.tsx`) that every tall piece stands in.

## Changing the cut

Edit `lead/Lead.tsx`: `SHOTS` is the list of shots with their frames, their act in the portrait teaser (`open`, `city`, `turn`, `hand`, `end`) and a React node; the pieces (`Bubble`, `Band`, `BriefChip`, `BigChip`, `Passbook`, `RouteCard`, `Sfx`) take their place in pixels of the 1920 by 1080 frame and their delay in frames, and lay themselves out in the 1080 by 1920 one by their zone. A `World` takes `tall`, the phone's recording of the same moment (its clip, in-point and speed) and its look: `scale` and `at` (the recording's top left corner in the frame) where the phone's view puts its people low, and `after`, another look from a frame of the shot on, where the recording cuts to another view. A bubble's `tall.head` is its speaker's head in the phone's recording (its own 1080 by 1920 pixels), or heads at frames of the shot where the speaker or the view moves; its tail stays on the head as the picture eases closer. A tall bubble lays its words in lines of known letters, so every box is known: `Lead.test.tsx` walks each shot at five frames and checks every piece stands in its zone. A change to the list shows in both. Render, pull frames with ffmpeg (or `--stills`), look, repeat. The words rules of the game hold here too: never slots, turn, banked, pages, days left, a day count or a percentage, and nothing about how the game is made.

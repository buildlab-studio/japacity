# Rehearsal: the stageshow teaser

The four scenes of `Stageshow` and `StageshowPortrait`, as the stageshow skill's verify.md asks a scene to be handed over: the gold it started from, what it holds, what was filmed, what the film said and what was not checked.

## Gold

- `phone-touch` for the phone in the hand: a phone app told as one continuous object, the touch cursor, a few beats to a payoff. Kept: the kit's `Phone` drawn at a size that reads (452 wide in the 540 by 960 frame, a gutter above and either side, its foot past the bottom), the touch cursor that shows only where it taps, taps that open a surface in place and a payoff that holds. Changed: the app is the game (its band, sheets, handset and apps in the game's tokens), and each scene plays once into the next rather than folding back.
- `payout-flow` for the month's results: a value that fills on its own clock and a payoff that holds. Kept: the slow fill of a bar beside its figure rolling with `Count`. Changed: the bar is the game's savings bar, the figures are the game's.
- The laptop's frame is the game in a browser (`Window` with its address): the closest layout is a whole window that fills the stage, the game's centred column in it as the game draws it on a laptop.

## Beats

- `door` (4.5 s): the deck of lives, the developer's card tapped and picked, the ways it opens settle in under it, Next pressed.
- `step` (8.6 s): the brief's chip tapped, the phone rises on Japa, the official page's card read and the phone's line over it, the phone put away, the giver's toast and the step's dot turning green.
- `month` (9.2 s): the results lead with the salary and what was kept, the savings bar fills toward the move, the ask's row opens the ask, Look closer finds the second sign.
- `ways` (4.3 s): the phone's home screen, Japa tapped, the plan and the other ways out.

Each scene plays once (`play="once"`): the cut fades from one to the next, so no scene loops back on screen.

## Captured

- Widths: each scene at its shown width (1920 for the laptop, 1080 for the phone) and at 390, where the laptop's stage draws the phone's design (its compact layout).
- Look: the day paint only.
- Driven: the compositions step each scene's virtual clock to the frame; the film's runs use the browser's own clocks.

## Gate

The last line of `film.mjs --gate` for each scene and width is in the report that hands this over.

## Review

A fresh-eye review of the rendered frames, and what each finding became:

- The laptop's window stands whole with an even margin. It stays whole: the game's sheets and its yellow action stand at the window's foot, so a bleed would cut what the story presses. It fills nine tenths of the frame, which the gate reads as the stage's ground.
- The door on a laptop is a 600 wide reading column in a wide window, the sky either side. That is the door as the game draws it on a laptop; its height already fills the window, so it cannot grow without cutting its action.
- The next life in the deck meets the column's edge: it fades out there rather than ending on a cut.
- Where the eye lands in the phone: the game's own other ways out and what is coming up, no filler.
- The home screen's wallpaper between the place and the apps is the game's own wallpaper, kept.
- The door on the phone: the life's picture takes the phone's taller size, as the game's does, so the card, its ways and the age card fill the screen.
- The salary's amount said twice is the game's own results card, kept.
- The laptop's arrow: it waits past the stage's edge before its first press and goes to the world beside the column after each one, never resting on words.
- The end card's dark then navy ground is the lead cut's lights-off joke, kept as it plays there.

## Not checked

- Reduced motion beyond the poster states each stage sets.
- Hover and keyboard: the scenes are filmed, not used.

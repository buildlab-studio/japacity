# Accounts: sign-in, the age screen, who may sign up and deletion

> Rests on `../design/01-concept.md` section 16, `../design/04-where-things-live.md` sections 2 and 5. Better Auth and Cloudflare docs read on 8 Oct 2026, linked below.
> Kinds: none in the simulation. Accounts live in `apps/server` and its database tables; the simulation never sees an account, an email or a country.

An account is a way back to one character. It is made with as little personal data as the sign-in needs, by an adult, from anywhere, and every player may do the same with others. Nothing here costs a player money and nothing here asks for a password.

## Rules: signing in

**account:sign-in-without-secret** An account is secured by Google, by a passkey, or by a code sent to an email address. There is no password and no username login: the password sign-in of the auth library is left off, so its routes refuse every request, and a username is a display name only (account:username-and-unconfirmed-email). An account may link more than one method. A player starts as a guest with no method at all (account:guest-and-secured-states) and secures the account later (account:guest-secures-the-life).

**account:email-code-is-short-lived** An email code is 6 digits, valid for 5 minutes, and allows 3 tries; a new code replaces the old one. A code is sent without the request waiting on the send, so the reply takes the same time whether the address has an account or not. The database keeps only a code's hash, so a read of the database yields no code that works.

**account:passkey-needs-signed-in-session** A passkey is registered only from a signed-in session, and is bound to the game's own domain as its relying party. On an account on the admin list (moderation:admin-view-for-admin-list) that already holds a passkey, a new one is registered only from a session opened with a passkey; any other session is refused. Every registration is written to the audit log (a hash of the account, the passkey, whether the account is an admin, and how the session was opened), and a notice is sent to the account's email without the reply waiting on the send. Without it, anyone who got into an admin's email could add a passkey of their own and reach the admin pages. NIST SP 800-63B-4 section 4.1.2.1 asks that binding a new authenticator needs authentication at the highest level the account already has, or the level the new one will be used at if that is lower, and that the subscriber is told by a way independent of the binding (section 4.6). The admin rule is stricter than the standard's letter: it takes only a passkey session, where the standard would take any authentication at that level. The relying party id is exactly the host of SITE_URL, `japacity.app` in production (the apex, never a subdomain) and `staging.japacity.app` on staging, so a staging passkey never signs in to production. A SITE_URL that is not a bare https origin is refused. A passkey is bound to its relying party id for good, so neither id changes once a real passkey exists.

**account:passkey-added-in-place** Adding a passkey needs a session opened within the last 10 minutes, the auth library's fresh age (account:player-deletes-account). From an older session the account sheet asks "To add a passkey, first confirm it is you." in place, with a code sent to the account's email, and "Use my passkey" where the account holds one (only that account's own passkeys answer, as account:player-deletes-account says); once confirmed, the passkey is made at once, and nobody signs out to add one. The phone's passkey list names the passkey with the account's email, which the auth library already gives it from the account; nothing new is stored for the name.

**account:passkeys-listed-and-removed** The sheet of a kept life lists its passkeys under their count, oldest first, each by its provider's name from its AAGUID ("Passkey" where the authenticator gives none, as an iPhone's does) and "Added" with the day, each with "Remove". Removing the last one asks first and names the way still left: "You can still sign in with Google." or a code sent to the email. A removal that leaves this phone's session standing says "Removed. Other phones are signed out."; one that ended this phone's session too takes the player to the start screen with the signed-out notice.

**account:passkey-offered-where-it-works** "Add a passkey" shows only where the browser can make one: it has its own authenticator or can use a phone nearby (`PublicKeyCredential.getClientCapabilities()`, or the platform authenticator alone where that call is missing), and never inside an in-app browser (account:guest-secures-the-life's list), whose passkeys would stay in that app. It carries the line "Sign in with your face, fingerprint or screen lock instead of a code." Where a kept life holds no passkey, the first time the sheet shows on a device it offers one instead, quietly, in its place on the sheet: "Next time, sign in with your face or fingerprint. Add a passkey?" with "Not now"; never a prompt over the game, and never again on that device. The passkey on the delete confirmation shows under the same check.

**account:passkey-errors-told-apart** A passkey prompt the player cancels (the browser's NotAllowedError, or an abort) says nothing on the account sheet and is told as nothing happened elsewhere, never as a refusal. A passkey this phone already holds for the account is told "This phone already has a passkey for this life."; a passkey the account no longer holds is told "That passkey was removed from your account. Sign in another way." and is named to the phone with `PublicKeyCredential.signalUnknownCredential` where the browser has it, so the phone stops offering it. A passkey of another account is told "That passkey belongs to another life. Use one made for this life."

**account:no-text-message-codes** No text-message codes: a code to a Nigerian number costs $0.22 to $0.39 (`../design/04-where-things-live.md` section 5).

**account:stand-ins-test-sign-in** On this machine, sign-in is tested with stand-ins that exist only in the test server's config: email codes are read back from the test server's stand-in for the send, since the database keeps only their hash (account:email-code-is-short-lived); Google sign-in goes to a stub provider served by the test server that signs in a fixed test identity; passkeys are made by a virtual authenticator in the browser tests; and a signed-in session for a unit test comes from the test utilities' `login`. A test proves the production config holds none of the four.

**account:session-is-secure-cookie** A session is a cookie that is `HttpOnly`, `Secure` and `SameSite=Lax`. Deleting the account (account:player-deletes-account), and removing a linked method, end every session of that account at once. Staging keeps Cloudflare Access in front of the whole host, an email login with its own 24 h cookie beside this session. A request that meets a login wall or a session ended outside the game (a redirect, an opaque redirect, a redirect to a `cloudflareaccess.com` host, a 401 or 403 with no JSON body, a page where JSON was expected) is a `wall` (engine:browser-authority-for-dev-only), never the game's `signed-out`. Only the game's Worker answers in JSON, so a 401 or 403 with a JSON body (the game's `signed-out`, the auth library's refusals) is the server's answer, never a wall. The first wall reloads the whole page once, so the login shows; the reload is marked in the tab's session storage, and the mark is cleared only by a reply of the game's that comes 3 s or more after it was set, so on a host walled in part a route that answers at once beside a walled one never sets off a loop of reloads. A second wall within a minute does not reload again: the card says "Your session ended: open the game again." with Reload. Without session storage nothing reloads and the card shows. The game's own 401 always carries `signed-out` in its body and goes to the account screens.

**account:dead-cookie-expired-on-first-read** A browser that sends a validly signed session cookie whose session is gone (a reset, a deletion, a clean-up, sessions ended elsewhere) has that cookie expired on the reply to the first request that reads its session, whatever the reply's status: the game's `signed-out` 401 included. The session is read once for each request, however many of its gates and its route ask. A reply that sets a session cookie of its own, as the guest start does after finding no live session, keeps its own: the browser ends with the new cookie, never with none.

**account:session-renewal-reaches-browser** A live session read more than a day after it was last renewed is renewed for its full life (account:guest-and-secured-states' 400 days), and the renewed cookie is set on the reply that read it, so the browser's cookie lasts as long as the session it opens.

**account:sign-out-ends-the-session** Signing out ends the session on the server before the player leaves: a sign-out that does not reach the server keeps the player in, with "Could not sign out. Check your connection and try again.", so the next load never opens the life again on a shared phone. A kept life's sheet also holds "Sign out on every phone", asked first with "This signs you out everywhere, this phone too.": it ends every session of the account on the server, then drops this browser's cookie, and the player leaves with the signed-out notice; a failure keeps the player in with the same line.

## Rules: guests and secured accounts

A player passes the age screen and the bot check, picks a username and plays at once. The life lives on that device until it is secured. Securing is asked for at moments of investment, never at the door.

**account:guest-and-secured-states** An account has two states, `guest` and `secured`. A guest account is created by the auth library's anonymous sign-in after the age screen (account:age-screen-refuses-minors) and the bot check (account:bot-check-on-guest-creation), with a username (account:username-and-unconfirmed-email) and nothing else: no email, no password. Its session is the only way back in, so the life exists only in that browser on that device until secured; the game says so plainly when the life starts and in the quiet reminder of account:guest-reminded-to-secure. A notice left by signing out or deleting shows on the first screen of the gate and goes when the player moves on to another screen. A dead session cookie never stops a new sign-in: a browser still holding a signed session cookie whose session is gone (a reset, a deletion, a clean-up, sessions ended elsewhere) starts a guest or signs in by code, Google or a passkey as a browser with no cookie would, while a live guest's cookie still links the new way in (account:guest-secures-the-life); if the character's start is still answered signed-out after the guest start, the page says the life did not start and the next Begin makes the guest again.

**account:social-needs-secured-account** A guest can play the whole single life, see the world, the news and the rates. Only a secured account can buy lottery tickets, appear on any board or public card, invite or be invited as a partner or sibling, send or read messages, or have its life restored on another device. Each of these is refused for a guest with the code `guest-only` and a detail that names the way to secure; the client shows the ask, never a dead end. The server enforces every one: a hidden screen is not enough. The boards are guarded at `/api/boards/*`, the paths the client reads; the board's rows read the account's own guest state, and securing lists the account's board rows again (city:board-line-and-ranking).

**account:guest-secures-the-life** Securing: a guest adds Google first, or a code sent to an email address; a passkey can be registered once the account is secured. Inside in-app browsers where Google is blocked (WhatsApp, X, Instagram, TikTok), Google is not shown and the emailed code is offered, with "open in your browser". The auth library's anonymous link moves the guest onto the secured account; the life, the saves, the clock and every store row move with it, and a test proves nothing is lost. Securing with a method that already has an account joins nothing and overwrites nothing: the player is told that method is taken and keeps the guest life; a test proves neither life changes. The asks come after the first chapter, before the visa decision, and before anything in account:social-needs-secured-account; never at the door, never before the early win (guide:nothing-asked-before-early-win). The keep-this-life ask waits while a sheet is open; a mission card or a chapter close counts as an open sheet, and so does a brief's card still waiting for the place's first frame: the city marks that wait in the document (`[data-brief-pending]`, chapter:first-chapter-mission-card) and the ask waits for the mark to go. Google is offered on Google's own standard button, wherever it is offered (the keep-this-life sheet, the ways back in, the settings), as Google's sign-in branding guidelines describe it: the G as Google gives it at the left, unchanged, "Continue with Google", the light theme on a light sheet and the dark on a dark one, the guidelines' sizes and Google Sans Medium; the sheet around it keeps the game's voice ("Keep this life", "Or with your email"). Once the life is kept, the sheet says only that: the heading "Kept", the one line "Done. This life is yours on any phone now." and its close, with no reason, no ways to keep and no "Not now". Google's trip comes back marked with its purpose (`?google=kept`, `?google=in`) or with `?error=`; kept with Google, the securing sheet opens in its Kept state as the code's does; as a Kept sheet closes, and when a kept life is signed back in to, a short line over the world for three seconds names the way; a code to the address of a kept life opens it whatever way it was kept, with no words about it, since that is how the game works; Google on an address kept by code joins it by the verified address; where the library refuses (`account_not_linked`) the player is pointed to the code in the game's words.

**account:username-and-unconfirmed-email** The unconfirmed email and the username. An email that has not been confirmed gives nothing and blocks nothing: it cannot sign in or recover, and it does not reserve the address; if its real owner confirms it later on any account, the unconfirmed claim elsewhere is dropped. The username is letters, digits and underscore, 3 to 20 characters, unique whatever the capitals, not on the reserved list (the game's name, admin, support, staff, official, moderator, japacity and their spellings) nor matching the blocked-words list, with one free change and then a wait of `account.renameDays` between changes; it is the account's handle, never the character's name, never a way in, and reportable like any free text (`moderation.md`). The server enforces all of it. The account sheet shows the username and the name as two labelled rows, so neither is guesswork.

**account:guest-floods-are-capped** Against floods of guests: the bot check before a guest life is created, and at most `account.guestsPerDevice` new guest lives from one device cookie and `account.guestsPerConnection` from one connection in `account.guestWindowHours` hours, refused with `too-many`.

**account:idle-guests-cleaned-by-hand** Guest lives are never removed by a schedule. Each life keeps its last-played time. A tool run by hand, `pnpm guests:clean --idle-days <n>`, lists and, with `--apply`, removes guest accounts with no play for the given days (90 is the working number, one setting, `account.guestIdleDays`), through the same removal as deletion (account:deleted-data-removed-within-month); a short page in the repository says what it does. Secured accounts are never removed by it.

**account:guest-reminded-to-secure** A guest sees a quiet reminder to secure the life, saying plainly that it lives only on this phone until secured. It is a line on the settings sheet and on the month report after the first chapter, never a countdown, never a figure that is not real. `GET /api/account` carries `ways` read-only (no token, no subject); Settings opens the account from one full row at its top (the initial, the name, @username, the way kept or "Only on this phone until kept"), never from a small icon; the sheet names the way with its address and the passkeys held.

**account:play-starts-without-email** The emailed code must never be on the way to starting play: creating a guest life sends no email, and the load test (`load.md`) covers the code send at the target rate separately.

**account:start-screen-over-live-city** Online, the first screen is the start screen over the live city. It holds, from the top, the two lights hanging over the scene (account:two-lights-and-wink-lines), then in the sheet: the game's lockup, small at its head; the hook in two rows (text:hook-not-explanation); the whole game in three steps, an icon and a word each with a small arrow between (Hustle with the Jobs app's briefcase, Papers with the Papers app's folder, Fly with the Japa app's plane); **Play** with its small second line "Free · no sign-up"; "I already have a life here" for a secured account (Google, or a code by email: account:guest-secures-the-life's order and its in-app-browser rule); and the two links (Terms of play, Privacy notice). The second line holds by this rule, account:age-screen-refuses-minors and account:guest-and-secured-states: nothing is signed up for to start, the only question is the date of birth, and the guest is made unseen at Begin. Nothing says Lagos in words: the scene behind says it. Play leads to the age screen as account:age-screen-refuses-minors says (three fields, nothing filled, no hint of the age; under 18 refused and remembered), then to the three-screen door (prologue:front-door-three-screens); the bot check runs unseen when Begin creates the guest life (account:bot-check-on-guest-creation); the username is filled in from the name on the page and can be changed there with its rule (account:username-and-unconfirmed-email), never on a screen of its own; the guest line is said once on the page (account:guest-reminded-to-secure's words). With the local authority there is no account and no start screen: the game opens at the door. The start screen and the age screen carry no title over the figure. The game's lockup (the seal with two bulbs on a wire and the word, `apps/client/public/brand/japacity-lockup-<ground>.svg`) sits small at the head of the start screen's sheet and, once each at the top with air around it, on the account screens: the ways back in, the keep-this-life sheet (account:guest-secures-the-life), the invite's page (pair:invite-link-single-use) and the full screen (server:signups-off-shows-full); the age screen carries none. The start screen's lockup is drawn for the ground under it, the light ground's by day and the dark ground's under the night paint, following the paint the root carries. Behind the start screen, the age screen and the ways back in stands the live city: the painted map of Lagos (decision 0026), settled over the family house in Surulere, shown where WebGL draws (`canDraw` from `shared/frontdoor/Gate`, asked once in `App.tsx`); the compound and the figure come back at the door. Where WebGL is missing the look's picture stands in. On a screen wider than a phone, the start screen's, the age screen's, the ways back in's and the full screen's sheet stands centred in the door's column of at most 600 px (`shared/ui/lively/SheetColumn`), with the scene filling the window around it; on a phone the column is the screen.

**account:two-lights-and-wink-lines** The two lights and the lines with a wink. A place that carries a line with a wink has two or three lines at most, each in content, and one is picked at random once per visit, drawn on the device, so a reload may show another; never from the player's data, never by a word a player could put in the address (a dev build alone has a hook that fixes the pick, for pictures), and never on bad news, a refusal, grief or money lost, which are said plainly (text:bad-news-never-joked); the lines leave room for those who stay. The start screen's three, verbatim, two rows each: "Last one out, / off the light.", "Not going? / Keep the light on." (the stayer's answer), "Japa is not by force. / How ready are you?". The two lights hang over the start screen from the top edge: one bulb for the lives at home and one for the lives abroad on one wire, each glowing with its share, home never drawn under 30% of the light (new lives start there, and some stay by choice); sparks run along the wire toward abroad and now and then one back; HOME and ABROAD with their counts, "Everyone playing, now" under them, and a dusk across the top so the glows read in daylight. It is a picture of everyone, never a task: nothing in it can be tapped, switched or paid, and nothing rewards it, so a line about the light is a caption, never an errand. The counts are public and name no one. `GET /api/lights` answers `{ ok, home, abroad, back }`, the whole game's and never a region's, each rounded to the nearest ten, made from the stored totals of analytics:nightly-game-counters alone. HOME and ABROAD are lives being played, not lives ever started. A nightly job on the 00:00 UTC cron, beside the other nightly jobs (`apps/server/src/jobs/lives.ts`), reads every character in the characters store a page at a time and counts each whose life has not ended (turn:finish-ends-the-life) and whose account still exists and still names it (account:one-character-per-account), by where it stands, as analytics:nightly-game-counters's `lives/playing` for the day just ended; it keeps, returns and logs no character or account, only counts. `GET /api/lights` reads home and abroad from the whole game's rows of the last day counted; back is the `place/returned` of this UTC month. A life on the road counts at home, since the road is no departure (road:road-route-has-no-figure: no `japa/departed`, never named) and the place rule has two places (place:new-character-at-home); the count keeps the road under its own key, so moving it is one line. While no nightly run has counted, figures made from the other totals stand in, and the reply has the same shape and says nothing different: abroad is every `japa/departed` less every `place/returned`, home every `lives/started` less those abroad. The route reads no session, and nothing of the asker enters the reply, the key or a log line; it counts against the address limit (server:anonymous-limit-by-address) and is kept in the server's cache by the hour and public to the browser until the hour ends (server:public-replies-cached). With no answer the city's own people stand in: the crowds content gives every place at home and every place abroad (city:residents-fill-to-target), with no caption that calls them players. The glows breathe slowly and flicker as a spark reaches them, moved by transform and opacity alone with no render loop; reduced motion holds them still and draws no sparks (world:motions-under-half-second).

**account:dedication-page-and-its-doors** The dedication page. A full screen reached from the lantern canoe on the map (world:lantern-canoe-on-map) and from the five taps on Settings' version line (city:credits-screen-attributions), and from nowhere else: never from the start screen, which keeps the two lights of account:two-lights-and-wink-lines alone. The page: night over the lagoon, strings of small lights held up by canoes, the dedication's light large on the top string with "soksonjr" under it (exactly so, lower case), the lit lights of account:name-a-light-for-yours along the strings, each whose name is approved carrying its name, the rest bare. The lights fill the two strings under the dedication's light first, then the strings over the water, then its own string, and on the strings a named light takes every other place with a bare one between, every other name a little lower, so two names are never neighbours; glow layers only for the dedication's light, the account's own and the named. At the top the number of lights and "on the water tonight" (the true count, never rounded; the singular is "1 light"); the count and the close stand still at the head of the page, the count on the sky's own colour, so while the form is filled and the page rises over the keyboard they stay in view. At the foot one line, "For Osoko Emmanuel, 1998 to 2026. From Al-Ameen.", kept in content beside the dedication line of city:credits-screen-attributions (`DEDICATION_SHORT`, never retyped in code), then "Sun re o."; then the one button "Light one for yours" when the account has no light, disabled while the water is being read, or the account's own light named on the string with its line (account:name-a-light-for-yours). The page loads in its own chunk on first opening (engine:first-download-has-budget) and reads `GET /api/dedication/lights` (server:dedication-routes) as it opens; offline, or when the read fails, it shows the dedication's light, the strings bare, no count, and the button closed with "The water is out of reach right now." On a phone at most 240 lights are drawn, the top strings as bulbs and the rest as dots receding into the dark, every glow by opacity alone and still under reduced motion (world:motions-under-half-second); the count says the true number whatever is drawn. The picture's colours are literal like the two lights' and the paper map's (world:colours-are-role-tokens). It is never counted (analytics:client-sends-interface-counts lists no key for it).

**account:name-a-light-for-yours** Light one for yours. The page's button names the unlit light beside the dedication's, in place and with no sheet; while naming, that light is labelled "yours". The form: "Who is it for?" (a name of at most four words and forty characters), "Years, if you want" (a first year and a last, each 1900 to the current year, the last never before the first, either alone), each its own box with the label inside the box above the value (no fieldset or legend, which Safari draws across the border), the hint under the boxes "Four words at most. Its name shows to everyone once it has been looked over.", and "Light it", which waits for a name. The limits live in one pure function shared by the client and the server (`packages/content/src/about/about.light.ts`, `checkLight`), and the server refuses what the client refuses: "A name first." is the server's `name-empty` said back, a `bad-light` refusal shows its problem, and no answer shows the out-of-reach line. "Light it" sends `PUT /api/dedication/light`: the light is kept with the account (guest or secured; it moves with the account when secured, account:guest-secures-the-life, and goes with the account's deletion), one per account, a second `PUT` replacing it and clearing its approval. From then the account's own light hangs on the page's top string beside the dedication's with its name and years under it, for that account alone, and under the foot "Yours is on the water. Its name shows to everyone once it has been looked over."; under that line "Put it out" is a quiet button, always shown, never offered on a tap, that sends `DELETE /api/dedication/light`, removes the light and brings the button back. Every other account sees the light at once, bare, in the count and on the strings, and sees its name only once it is approved (moderation:dedication-lights-await-approval); a refused name puts the light out and the account may light again. A name reaches other players only through that approval and is typed nowhere else, which is why it is not contact between players under region:no-stranger-matching. The page's words live with the start screen's words (text:jokes-keep-a-plain-version) and name no real person but the friend the game is dedicated to. The privacy notice says the name and years a player types are kept with the account and shown to other players once looked over. No light is kept on the device alone: a `japacity.door.light` left in a browser's storage is ignored.

## Rules: who may sign up

**account:same-contact-for-everyone** Every account has the same contact with other players, wherever it signed up: preset phrases and pair records only between accounts connected by an invite, with block and report (region:same-contact-everywhere). There is no table of regions and no setting per country.

**account:country-comes-from-cloudflare** A request's country is the one Cloudflare gives it (`request.cf.country`, the same as the `CF-IPCountry` header). The country at sign-up is stored on the account as given, `XX` when Cloudflare gives none (`T1` is Tor). It counts play by country (`analytics.md`) and chooses the advert region (advert:campaign-chosen-without-player-data), and decides nothing a player may do.

**account:sign-up-open-everywhere** Sign-up is open from every country: the age screen, its standing and the guest start answer a request the same wherever it comes from.

**account:age-screen-refuses-minors** The age screen comes before the username and the guest life (there is no sign-in method at the door) and asks for a date of birth with no date filled in and no hint of the age needed. Under 18 is refused, and a cookie that lasts 30 days makes the screen refuse that browser again without asking. The account stores that the screen was passed, its date and the method `declared`, never the date of birth itself, because less data kept is less to protect.

**account:bot-check-on-guest-creation** A bot check runs once, when a guest life is created (that is when the account is created): the client sends a Cloudflare Turnstile token, the server checks it with Turnstile's verify endpoint before the account exists, and a failed or reused token makes no account, and a pass is accepted only when Turnstile's `hostname` is SITE_URL's host, so a token solved on another site is refused. Signing in again needs no bot check. Tests use Turnstile's published test keys, which pass or fail by design, and the dummy token they accept. For the local stack (server:production-and-staging), a pass whose verify answer carries `metadata.result_with_testing_key` set to true, which Cloudflare always reports as solved on `example.com`, is accepted only when SITE_URL's host is `localhost` or `127.0.0.1`; on every other host the reported host must match. Following Turnstile's server-side validation guidance, the widget is rendered with the action `guest`, and a pass that is not a test key's is accepted only when the verify answer's `action` is `guest` (refused `action-mismatch`); a test key's pass names no action and is judged by its host alone, as above. A token longer than 2048 characters, the longest Turnstile issues, is refused `token-too-long` before any call, and a verify answer that is not a JSON object fails closed as `bad-reply`.

**account:sign-in-routes-rate-limited** Sign-in routes are rate limited by the auth library, with its store in the database, not in memory, so the limit holds across isolates: at most `auth.limits.otpSendPer60s` code sends per address and per email in 60 seconds, at most `auth.limits.otpSendPerDay` (10) per email in a day, so one address cannot be flooded with codes, and at most 3 code checks per 10 seconds per address. The email codes have their own load test (`tools/load/codes.ts`), with Google sign-in as the main path. The provider is Cloudflare Email Service (decision 0007). The codes and the notices leave through one seam (`apps/server/src/mail/mail.ts`), which tries in order: with `MAIL_TRANSPORT=mailpit`, which only the local stack sets (server:production-and-staging), each is posted to Mailpit's HTTP send API on this machine, its subject and body from content's words, and refused when Mailpit's address is not on this machine; otherwise the binding when it is bound; otherwise the seam refuses every send, so a deployment without the binding sends nothing. Production and staging each bind the provider as `MAIL`, a `send_email` binding in `apps/server/wrangler.jsonc` allowed one sender only, the game's `game@mail.japacity.app` (display name Japacity) on the sending subdomain `mail.japacity.app`, kept in one constant (`SENDER` in the seam) that a test holds equal to the binding's list. Every mail carries `Reply-To: hello@japacity.app`, a monitored inbox, since the sending subdomain has none (`REPLY_TO` beside `SENDER`). Every mail has a text part and a plain HTML part saying the same. A send the binding refuses rejects with the binding's error code alone, never its message, the address or the code (server:secrets-stay-on-server); the auth library logs that rejection and the route answers as it would, since its reply never waits on the send (account:email-code-is-short-lived). The binding needs no secret. Workers Paid includes 3,000 sends a month, then a charge per thousand; a new account starts on a small daily quota that grows with good sending; a mail is at most 5 MiB and 50 recipients.

## Rules: one character, and leaving

**account:one-character-per-account** One account holds one character at a time; a new one is made only after the old is ended by account:start-a-new-life (a second while one lives is refused `one-character`).

**account:start-a-new-life** Settings holds "Start a new life". One sheet names the character and their age and says plainly that this life ends and cannot be brought back; Cancel is the easy choice and the ending button is held to confirm (no typing). On confirming, the life ends: locally the save is cleared and the kept person with it; online the account's character is ended by its own route (the life's rows are retired as a deletion's are, account:deletion-keeps-anonymous-totals, the account itself stays with its age pass and its ways in), a real sibling or partner joined to the old life is told in the game's words (`people/ended` with its own `why`, `restart`) and their slot returns to a computer character; then the front door opens, filled in as onboarding is. No family member or invite is needed. The family draw is the new life's own roll (prologue:tile-path-open-from-start retries seeds for the tile), never kept from the old one. Online, a guest's restart makes the new life under the same guest account with the same username (account:username-and-unconfirmed-email's rename rule untouched); the bot check is not asked again.

**account:player-deletes-account** A player deletes the account from the settings, confirming with a session signed in within the last 10 minutes or a fresh email code. Signing in again to confirm offers a passkey only where the account holds one, and only one of the account's own passkeys confirms: the phone's answer is checked against the account's passkeys before it is sent, so another account's passkey on the same phone opens no session and deletes nothing. At once: every session ends, the character leaves every place and pair (the other player is told only that they left), and the account can no longer sign in.

**account:deleted-data-removed-within-month** Within 30 days of the confirmation, and never later than one month, the server removes: the account and its methods (Google link, passkeys, email), sessions, the character row, every save segment, snapshot and log row, the turn clock, its messages on both sides (`../design/04-where-things-live.md` section 4), its rate-limit rows, its country and age records, and its ledger marks.

**account:deletion-keeps-anonymous-totals** Deletion keeps only what names no one: the day totals of the money report, a lottery draw's ticket count and its published list of anonymous ticket tokens (lottery:fingerprint-commits-secret), and an audit row holding a one-way hash of the account id and the date of deletion, so a second request can be answered.

**account:backups-never-restore-deleted** Backups are put beyond use: no restore brings a deleted account back into play, and a restore re-runs every deletion recorded in the audit rows since the backup was taken. The privacy notice says how long the provider keeps backups. The restore window is up to 7 days, and the privacy notice says so.

**account:only-needed-data-kept** The account keeps only what sign-in and these rules need: the email address where one is used, the Google subject id and email where Google is used, passkey public keys, the sign-up country, the age record of account:age-screen-refuses-minors, and the created date. No name, phone number, address or photo is asked for.

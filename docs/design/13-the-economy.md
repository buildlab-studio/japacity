# The economy

**Context and scope.** What a life at home can earn, how fast it climbs, how it learns, and how the money reaches a way out: the trades' ladders and side jobs, the yearly naira pay review, learning tech in bands, the family's gift for Canada, and the student's money abroad, with the real fees the routes charge. The ways out cost what they really cost (`../rules/route.md`, `../rules/route-gates.md`, `../rules/status.md`); this page is about the income side and the money flows that meet them. The rules are on `../rules/activity.md` (activity:each-trade-climbs-its-ladder to activity:bootcamp-paid-after-a-job), `../rules/quality.md` (quality:skill-bands-slow-gains), `../rules/streets.md`, `../rules/document.md` (document:trade-papers), `../rules/route-gates.md` (route-gates:family-gift-counts-with-deed, route-gates:canada-funds-checked-at-departure, route-gates:nurses-sponsored-while-care-shut, route-gates:pool-draws-invite), `../rules/route.md` (route:family-pledge-pays-tuition, route:education-loan-pays-the-school), `../rules/abroad-money.md` (abroad-money:tuition-paid-in-instalments, abroad-money:missed-tuition-runs-in-stages), `../rules/status.md` (status:course-passed-or-withdrawn, status:graduate-visa-applied-for, status:renewing-a-status), `../rules/place.md` (place:december-factor-from-the-real-median), `../rules/director.md` (director:education-draw-opens-for-a-time) and `../rules/scenario.md` (scenario:economy-meets-its-targets). The decisions are 0031 (each trade climbs its own ladder), 0032 (learning in bands) and 0033 (the student's money flow). For game design. Not immigration or financial advice.

**Goals.**

- Every trade reaches a way out that fits it by its own work, in a playable time: a careful player who stays in the trade flies inside the trade's band in section 2.
- Loyalty to a trade has a value: a rung above, a side job that belongs to it, and naira pay that keeps some pace with the dollar.
- Tech stays the richest road, as it is in life, but it costs time or money like any other, and pivoting to it is a third choice, never the answer for everyone.
- A student who really holds the tuition completes; one who shows only borrowed money meets warnings with a way back at each, and can still fail.
- Real figures are the floor: every fee and pay figure has its real value and source, or says "working value", and a factor needs a reason.

**Non-goals.**

- A cost factor below real on the routes' fees or funds: the gaps are on the income side (section 10).
- Borrowed money for Canada's funds: IRCC refuses it.
- Money that tops itself up: every catch-up road is chosen and has a price.
- A UK teacher's route. From 9 Sep 2026 teachers trained in Nigeria cannot use the "Apply for qualified teacher status in England" service, so the game builds none; the teacher's levers are the trade's own ladder, Canada's education draw and the master's.
- A cap on months or a timer: the targets are tuned to a time, never enforced by a limit.

## 1. The decision in one line

Each trade climbs its own ladder with a side job of its own and a yearly naira review, tech is learned in bands that slow as they rise, the family's gift for Canada counts with its deed, and the student pays half at enrolment and the rest monthly, with every miss a warning before a withdrawal.

## 2. The targets per trade

Months to the first flight for a careful player who stays in the trade, without luck, in the golden world. The money is the months of surplus to the route's money, starting money included; the papers add their own waits (the passport, the tests, a school's answer, the verification, the pool). Reading the table:

- **The teacher is the hardest trade, on purpose.** Lagos pays teachers the least of the graduate trades: ₦25,000 to ₦250,000, with ₦100,000 a common figure. The teacher's faster ladder (the senior rung at 18 months, the head of department at 30) and home lessons at up to ₦120,000 bring the Canada road to about 60 months with the family's gift; the director's education draw is the lever that can bring it sooner.
- **The master's is the dearest road for every naira trade.** About ₦49.3m in all at the start rates, about $37,000, which is real. It is reached through the family (the pledge, which pays the school), a dollar rung, the land, or the education loan. It is no one's first road below the senior bank officer.
- **The tech pivot.** A non-developer who pivots by the free path flies at about month 45 to 50 (section 5): inside every trade's dearer band and slower than every trade's cheap one.
- **A crash** (world-b, the pound 2.36 times dearer in 36 months). The Gulf and the UK nurse's route stay reachable in 7 to 22 months of money. Every formal route priced in foreign money runs away from naira pay even with the review. That is honest for a crash: the dollar rungs, remote support and the remote developer, are what survive it, as in 2023 and 2024.

## 3. The principles

| | The principle | What games and writers on game economies do | How it lands here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every source flows to a sink the player wants, and the sinks run a little larger | Daniel Cook: value chains end at a motivation, and a chain with no reachable end makes early actions feel meaningless; sinks a little larger than sources | Each trade's rungs point at a way out its own surplus reaches. The passbook already shows the target; the ladders mend the chain |
| 2 | Tune to a time to goal, not to a hard limit | Ian Schreiber, Game Balance Concepts, level 7: tune the gains, the loops and the phase lengths so the game ends in the intended time; hard limits are the lazy answer | Section 2 sets months to the first flight by trade; the balance test holds them. No cap on months, no timers |
| 3 | Costs climb faster than income, so each next step takes longer | Anthony Pecorella, the math of idle games: costs grow faster than production until a milestone resets the pace | Learning bands: each band gives less a month. A rung is the milestone that resets the pace |
| 4 | Many roads; the strongest arrives late and costs an investment | Stardew Valley's best earners need the greenhouse or rare seeds; The Sims 4's promotions ask several skills at once | Tech can stay the richest road, but its remote job takes about as long as a trade's own dearer route, and each trade's own first way out stays faster than pivoting |
| 5 | Every career is a ladder with readable unlocks | The Sims 4's career levels list the skills each needs; Football Manager's contracts carry yearly rises that compound | Every trade has two or three rungs, each unlocked by months and one quality or paper, shown in the Jobs app; a yearly review gives loyalty a value |
| 6 | Catch-up the player controls | Schreiber: help for the player behind feels artificial when the player has no control over it; Civilization VI's boosts cut a technology's cost when the player does the linked thing | The poorer start gets more roads, each chosen and priced: the pledge or the gift (standing, the family's stake), the savings circle, the free path (time and discipline), the payment plan (a fee). Nothing tops money up by itself |
| 7 | Failure is a sequence of warnings with a way back, never a cliff | The Sims 4 cuts the power after unpaid bills rather than sending the repo man at once | Tuition: a reminder, the access cut with a plan, a final warning, withdrawal, then two months to put it right (obligation:way-out-at-every-stage) |
| 8 | No single dominant strategy | Schreiber and Romero, Game Balance (2021), on transitivity and cost curves; balancing progression with simulated players | The balance test's fourth measure: among lives that did not start as developers, at most 35% of flights leave from a developer rung, and no route takes over half the formal flights |
| 9 | Model before you ship, and keep the model in the loop | Economy sheets that simulate time, money and progression | A first model for the tuning; simulated play is the proof (scenario:economy-meets-its-targets) |
| 10 | Real figures are the floor; a factor needs a reason | The game's own rule pages | Factor 1.0 wherever a source exists; factors only to keep the dearer schools dearer, the December peak week, and where a page says why |

## 4. The ladders and the yearly review

**Ladders.** Every trade at home gets two or three rungs and one side job tied to it (activity:each-trade-climbs-its-ladder). The way up is months across the trade's jobs plus a quality or a paper of the trade: a nursing grade, the teaching registration (`document.trcn`), the chartered exams (`document.ican`, three sittings over 18 months), capital from the savings circle for the fish trade. The pay is the real advertised pay, after tax, at factor about 1.0 (activity.md, Numbers):

- **Nurse**: staff nurse ₦180,000, senior nursing officer ₦280,000 (30 months, care 70), ward manager ₦420,000 (60 months, care 80, standing 50). Side job: home-care visits at weekends.
- **Teacher**: private school ₦90,000, senior teacher at a better school ₦220,000 (18 months, English 60, the registration), head of department ₦400,000 (30 months, a master's at home or standing 60). Side job: home lessons, the exam coaching tier for a teacher of two years.
- **Customer service**: agent ₦110,000, team lead ₦220,000 (18 months, English 60), remote support for a foreign firm $500 (24 months across support work, English 70, tech 20). Side job: night support, tied to the trade, since at $350 for any life at English 50 and tech 20 it would pay more than a bank officer earns and be a second road to the dollar for everyone.
- **Shop**: phone sales ₦70,000, shop supervisor ₦120,000, your own stall. Side job: weekend phone repairs.
- **Fish**: the racks ₦70,000, your own kiln ₦120,000 (₦100,000 for the kiln and the first stock), fish trader ₦200,000 (₦300,000 of capital). Side job: canoe deliveries. The capital is paid, as it is in life, where 85% of Makoko's women fishers fund their trade through a savings collector (activity:capital-opens-own-work).
- **Banker**: bank officer ₦320,000, senior ₦560,000, relationship manager ₦900,000 (36 months as senior, network 50). Side job: a POS stand in a cousin's name, ₦300,000 for the machine and the float.
- **Accountant**: accounts officer ₦200,000, chartered accountant ₦400,000 (24 months, the chartered exams), finance manager ₦700,000. Side job: bookkeeping for small firms.
- **Developer**: the junior job asks a piece of work to show (portfolio 1) beside tech 30, since employers judge the portfolio.

**The yearly review** (activity:naira-pay-reviewed-yearly). At each 12 months in a naira job, pay is reviewed against the official dollar: a formal employer gives 10% plus half of the dollar's rise beyond 10% over the year, at most 30%; an informal one (the private school, the shop counter, the racks) 5%; own work keeps its own growth. Dollar pay is not reviewed: the naira it buys moves with the world. Real: Zenith raised all staff 20% to 30% in Jan 2025 and four banks' wage bill rose about 13% a head in 2025, against inflation of 15% to 25%; small employers rarely review, and low-end private schools pay below the minimum wage. So a calm year adds a little real pay, and a crash still pushes naira pay down against the pound, as 2024 did. The review reads the dollar, the variable that moves the routes' price.

## 5. Learning

A ₦5,000 day of class that gives +2 and repeats as long as the body allows would make tech the escalator for everyone: tech 30 and the junior developer's ₦320,000 one month and about ₦75,000 away for any life. Real learning costs time or money.

- **Bands** (quality:skill-bands-slow-gains): starting, junior-ready (30), working (50), strong (70), senior (85). Every tech study at home names its gain a month for each band, and the gains shrink as the bands rise.
- **The day's class is capped** (activity:study-days-capped): three a month, which together give what the free path's month gives.
- **The free path** (activity:free-path-keeps-a-streak): free sites and videos for the month's data, ₦6,500. +3, +2, +1 a month by band, nothing from 70, where real skill comes from work. Discipline is a streak with no draw: every fifth month in a row finishes a certificate. Junior-ready in 10 months, inside the real 9 to 15.
- **The paid paths** (activity:paid-tech-courses): a short course (₦45,000), a year's diploma (₦40,000 a month), a full-time bootcamp paid after a job (₦3,000,000 over 36 months once a developer job is held, activity:bootcamp-paid-after-a-job) and a free internship for the junior-ready. One tech study at a time.
- **The apprenticeship** at Computer Village stops at tech 30 and gives +2 (streets:apprentice-learns-repair): a hardware trade, not a free road to tech 60.

A teacher who pivots by the free path is junior-ready at month 9 or 10 and hired at about month 12; the job and the path give tech 50 by about month 17 and 60 by about 21; the mid-level rung at about month 36 after its 24 months; strong (75) at about 33; the remote hunt lands at about month 40 to 45, and Canada's funds follow about 5 months later. The teacher flies at about month 45 to 50, inside the trade's dearer band, and the life abroad is richer: the trade-off a pivot should be.

## 6. The family's gift for Canada

The held-funds loan and the pledge belong to the master's, so without a family road Canada is funded from own savings alone. The family's gift is the real road (route-gates:family-gift-counts-with-deed): the family meeting, called for a Canada plan once the credential assessment is held, gives family means times ₦100,000 (₦3,000,000 at means 30), with a notary's gift deed that says it is not a loan. The family's stake rises, so it is the gift or the master's pledge, never both. The gift is the applicant's to spend, and the funds are asked at the pool, at the invitation and again at the flight (route-gates:canada-funds-checked-at-departure), so spending it shuts the door until it is replaced. In the model the gift brings the nurse to 58 months rather than 64, the teacher to 60 rather than 66 and the banker to 42 rather than 46.

## 7. Students abroad

A first tuition instalment of about £3,667 to £4,334 in month 1, against a part-time surplus of £400, with held funds gone back to the lender at the flight and a pledge that cannot be spent, breaks 81% of simulated students. Most Nigerian students in life complete and go on: 20,604 Graduate extensions to Nigerians in the year to Jun 2026, against about 19,000 to 30,000 study grants a year before. The ones who fail are the ones without the money they showed. So:

1. **Half at enrolment, then four monthly instalments** (abroad-money:tuition-paid-in-instalments), as Sunderland and Greenwich run.
2. **The pledge pays the school** (route:family-pledge-pays-tuition): the parent's account the visa counted is the account the fees come from.
3. **A missed payment is a sequence** (abroad-money:missed-tuition-runs-in-stages): a reminder; the access cut and a payment plan; a final warning; withdrawal only after two plan payments missed in a row; then two months, the real 60 days, to pay and be reinstated, take a new course or switch (status:course-passed-or-withdrawn).
4. **Work as the rules allow** (status:status-sets-work-rule): 20 hours in term, full time in the Christmas and Easter vacations, the summer at 20 hours for the dissertation, and any job once the course has ended.
5. **The Graduate visa costs what it costs** (status:graduate-visa-applied-for): £937 and the surcharge, paid from the full-time months after the course.
6. **Warnings at the source and a regulated loan** (offer.md, route:education-loan-pays-the-school): the lender's show-money says on its card that it goes back at the flight and the fees stay due; a regulated education loan pays the school direct and is repaid from the Graduate status.

In the model (Northgate at a second upper, £11,000 of tuition left, £1,003 a month of lived costs): a careful student holding the full £21,539 shown completes, pays the Graduate visa in month 13 and has £10,203 left; a pledged student with £8,000 of their own completes with £554; one with £5,000 misses once, takes the plan and completes; one holding £9,000 is withdrawn in month 7; one who arrived on borrowed money with £300 is withdrawn in about month 4 or 5 and has the two months after. So the rule to tell the player is plain and true: hold the tuition left, and work pays the living.

## 10. Alternatives considered

- **A cost factor below real on the fees and the funds.** Halving Canada's funds or the master's would still leave the nurse at about 87 months for Canada and the junior developer at about 110 for the master's: the gaps between home pay and the routes' money are 3 to 50 times, and a factor cannot close that. It would also teach false figures. The fix is on the income side, at real pay.
- **A loan for Canada's funds.** Opening the lender's show-money to the points route would be the cheapest fix in content, but IRCC says the money cannot be borrowed from another person, and a game that teaches borrowed funds teaches a refusal. The family's gift with its deed is the real road.
- **Withdrawal at the first default.** Teesside's withdrawals of 2024 show it can happen, but it breaks four in five simulated students, careful ones with a pledge among them, where life sees most complete; the multi-step policies the universities publish, which Teesside was told it had not followed, are the real sequence.
- **Making being noticed a yearly raise.** The content-light way to grow pay. It would reward a draw, not loyalty, and would ignore the dollar, the variable that moves the routes' price; the review reads it.
- **A cap on tech alone, or halving tech gains from 50.** Each stops the escalator but leaves no road a player chooses between: free, paid or full time. The bands and the paths give the choice a price in months or in naira.

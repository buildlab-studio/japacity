# London

**Context and scope.** Why life in the UK is drawn as it is: London's places, its ways and fares, the costs of a month lived by hand, and its painted map (`apps/client/src/features/map/paper/london.tsx`). **Goals.**

- Show players what the life abroad they aim for is like: hard, but not hopeless.
- Spend every pound abroad where it would really be spent: at the stop, the till, the cooking corner.
- Give every job abroad a place to go to.

**Non-goals.** Any change to the rules of money, visas, jobs and the family; London as a whole city (the map shows south-east London, with Heathrow on an inset); real business names on shops and workplaces (city:invented-names-for-risky-businesses).

## 1. Summary

Life abroad is lived the way life in Lagos is: in places with things to tap, on rides paid at the stop, with food bought and cooked, and in a workplace the character goes to. The rules of money, visas, jobs and the family are the same everywhere; only the world around them is drawn.

## 2. Why, and the pattern for every country abroad

Lagos is lived by hand: a meal costs money when it is eaten, a ride when it is taken, and a job has a door. Abroad works the same way, so the player meets abroad as a place to be in, not as a sum. Every country abroad follows this pattern:

- **A painted map** of the part of the country where the life is lived, folding and opening as Lagos's does (world:painted-maps-abroad).
- **Rides paid at the stop**, in the country's own ways and vehicles (world:ride-abroad-and-workplace). Where the country has a pass, it is bought once a month at the station's machine, because nobody pays a month's fares at once (place:country-pass-covers-ways).
- **Food bought and cooked**, paid as it is used. Besides the rent, the only fixed monthly charge is the phone (abroad-money:arrival-opens-upkeep-and-room).
- **A workplace with a door** for every job: the door or desk where the job is got and held, the rota board for overtime, the manager's door for the next rung, and a colleague with a line (world:ride-abroad-and-workplace). The job takes its days by itself as the month plays; the place is where the character goes to get the job, to be seen, and to hear things.
- **A community place**, a church or a corner, where the people a life can meet are found (world:nobody-picked-from-list).
- **The bag by the room's door**, where the way home and the December flight are taken (world:japacity-bag-by-door).
- **The street find**: something interesting seen on the street that the player may stop to look at (streets:street-finds-abroad).
- **Positions rough on purpose**, with made-up spots marked as such (city:positions-rough-on-purpose).

Everything else (visas, the ladders, the credit file, rent, money sent home, December and the return) works as [Abroad](../site/plays/abroad.md) describes.

## 3. What a player sees in London

1. **A painted map of south-east London**: the river across the top, the railway through Peckham, the roads, the district names, and every built place as its own sticker. Heathrow is too far west for the map, so it sits on a corner inset with a plane (world:painted-maps-abroad).
2. **The red bus and the train.** The bus is one flat fare whatever the distance and the train is priced by distance, as London's are. A ride plays inside a red bus or a train carriage. The bus pass covers the bus only, so the train is still paid per trip.
3. **Food bought and cooked.** The big shop on the high street sells the weekly shop, which fills the belly as a pot does at home, and the room's cooking corner cooks a meal. Rye Lane has the chicken shop, the Nigerian shop (a taste of home, dearer), the barber, the clothes shop, the market stalls and the pub. The phone is on pay as you go.
4. **Workplaces with doors**: the care home, the ward, the office, the warehouse, the big shop's shop floor and the chicken shop's kitchen.
5. **The church** on the Old Kent Road is the Nigerian community's place.
6. **The test centre** is a London place, so the nurse's tests and the driving test happen somewhere on the map. **Heathrow** is where the character arrives and where the December flight leaves.

## 5. Ways and fares

The game's fares and paces are in the Numbers of [`place.md`](../rules/place.md). They rest on London's 2025 fares, rounded: the bus hopper fare £1.75, a monthly bus and tram pass about £95, and a zone 1 to 6 off-peak single about £5.60.

- The bus is one flat fare, as London's hopper fare is, and the pass makes it free for the rest of the month.
- The train is priced by distance, so the long ride to Heathrow costs what an off-peak single does.
- Walking works as at home, including the walk home when no fare can be paid (world:ride-sheet-shows-fares).
- Lagos's fuel scarcity does not apply abroad.

## 6. The costs of a London month

Each cost is a priced act done on the spot, in pounds, where it would really be paid: the weekly shop at the big shop, a meal in the room, the chicken shop, the Nigerian shop, a pint, the barber, clothes and the stalls on Rye Lane (streets:london-life-by-hand, streets:street-shopping-abroad). The room and the phone are the two monthly charges ([`abroad-money.md`](../rules/abroad-money.md)). The figures are in the Numbers of [`streets.md`](../rules/streets.md) and [`abroad-money.md`](../rules/abroad-money.md).

**Worked example.** A survival job in the warehouse pays about £23,000 a year before tax, about £1,670 a month after it. After the room, the bus pass, four weekly shops and a few evenings out, about £665 is left for money home, the credit file and the December flight. Hard, but not hopeless.

## 7. Alternatives considered

- **One monthly figure abroad.** Food, transport and the phone as one sum a month, a map of two stops, and jobs found at a notice with no place to go to. Cheaper to build, but it turns abroad into a sum and a list; drawn places make it a life.
- **Bus fares paid once a month.** Nobody pays a month's fares at once: a pass is tapped on the bus each day. So the pass is bought once a month at the station's machine, and the train is still paid per trip.
- **The big shop once a month.** Nobody shops for a whole month at once, so the weekly shop repeats, as the pot does at home.
- **London's whole map.** Every borough at the map's scale would leave Peckham too small to tap. South-east London on the map, with Heathrow on an inset, keeps every stop tappable.

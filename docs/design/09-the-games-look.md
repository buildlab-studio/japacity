# The game's look

**Context and scope.** The look of the interface over the world: its surfaces, type, pictures, the fan of acts, the briefs, how much people say, and how things move and sound. The game is visual and light, never serious and dense. The structures it dresses, the world, the map, the phone and the lived month, are drawn in `07-the-world-first.md` and `08-the-lived-month.md`. Decision record: `../decisions/0025-the-games-look.md`. The code is the system and the night paint in `apps/client/src/index.css`, the fan and the brief in `apps/client/src/features/city/fan/` and `brief/`, the cues in `apps/client/src/shared/sound/cues.ts`, and the market and buy mode (decision 0019).

**Goals.**

- One light, friendly system on every surface, day and night.
- A picture on every choice, and words of a few at a time.
- Lagos lives in the world and the words; the interface gets out of the way.

**Non-goals.** Chatter in the corner tied to nobody; a document look (patterned headers, small capitals, hairline rows, buttons with black edges, a rule's words on screen); the month's close as a statement of account; a second theme; screens that compare one player with another; a page view; a fly-over in place of the scene.

## 1. The decision in one line

One light, friendly system over the world: pale sky surfaces, white cards, navy ink, danfo yellow for the one action; a picture on every choice, cut as a sticker from our own models; titles of a few words in Rubik and one line in our voice; cost, time and effect as tilted sticker chips; the acts of a thing fanned out of the thing itself in the scene; the same shapes in navy at the world's night. Lagos lives in the world and the words; the interface gets out of the way.

## 2. The system

- **Surfaces.** The sheet is pale sky (`--sheet`, #eaf3fb), cards are white with a 20 px radius, a thin navy edge and a soft shadow; the ink is navy; chips, the small labels for a cost, a time or an effect, are tinted pills; the one action is danfo yellow with a 4 px navy underside that flattens on press. No sand or beige anywhere; no pattern on everyday surfaces. The adire circle pattern shows only on the travel data page, the moment card's header and the account door's band.
- **Night.** When the world's clock (clock:game-day-on-real-time) says night, the same shapes repaint: navy surfaces, cream ink and edges, the yellow action unchanged. One system, two paints, driven by what already drives the sky.
- **Type.** Rubik, self-hosted, in three sizes: 22 px for titles and money, 16 for body, 12 for chips; sentence case; tabular figures for money. The Danfo display face is on the share cards only; the wordmark is drawn as paths. No tracked capitals, handwriting, chalk or monospace outside the data page and a slip.
- **Pictures.** Every choice, thing, place and reward carries a picture: a render of the game's own model where one exists (`tools/assets/shoot.ts`, the `tile` view), cut as a sticker with a white edge and a slight tilt; an emoji stands in where no model exists, and `apps/client/src/shared/tiles/missing.ts` lists those stand-ins. People are shown by a name pill with a flat mark, never by a face render.
- **Tints by kind.** A round act button and a tile take a soft tint by what the act is: food peach, study blue, money green, body lilac, people rose; everything else stays the sheet's tint.
- **Words.** A title of at most four words, one line under it in the speaker's voice (`../rules/text.md`), never the rule's words; cost, time and effect as chips ("₦1,500", "A morning", "+Belly"); a locked act says why in three words.
- **Chips earn their place**: a chip says only what is not obvious. A price shows only where there is one; "Free" shows only on an act a player would expect to pay for (a meal, a ride), never on sleep, a bath or water, and never beside a "No cost" that says the same. The time shows only when it is more than a morning (a day, a weekend, a week), never "a morning" on every card. An effect chip is at most two per card and never repeats the one line. The fan carries the price pill alone: the balance before struck through and the balance after belong to a sheet where there is room (the ride sheet, a purchase), not to a round button in the scene; the band's money counter is the balance, and while a fan button is pressed the counter shows what would be left, then settles back if the press is released. No colour key on screen: the tints speak for themselves or they are not needed.
- **Facts, in one order.** An act's facts are tilted sticker chips, each an icon and two or three words, always in the order of `FACT_ORDER` (`apps/client/src/shared/words/facts.ts`): pay with a coin, chance, time with a clock, when it pays or answers with a calendar, a bill over months with a receipt, a fee with a tag, a rate with two arrows, where it counts with a plane, and a catch with a warning sign. The chance is a word with a small three-bar gauge beside it (`ChanceGauge`): the bars carry the shape, the word is always there, and colour never speaks alone. What an act changes is an arrow tag (`ToneTag`): a gain with an up arrow, a cost to the body or the mood with a down arrow in a person's words ("tires you", "wears you"). What it needs is a mark, a tick when met and a cross when not. The one line above the chips holds only what no chip says. A choice of ways (the ride sheet, Send money home) is a row of picture tiles, the picked one in the pick colour, with the picked way's facts as chips under the row. Where a chip does not work, a long fact that cannot be two or three words or the dense lines inside a card that lists several things, the same facts stand as icon rows, each a small icon on a soft square and its words (`Facts`' rows). A check against a target is a bar with both figures under it and a stamp with the verdict (`CheckMeter`).
- **One action.** One main button a surface, the verb in one or two words and its price where it has one ("Apply" under the job named, "Sit exam · ₦120,000", "Let's go"), never the title above it again; a button with no title over it (the fan's, a row's) keeps the full name. Where every card is a tap (the fan, the market), there is no button: the card is the tap and the price is a yellow pill on it, with the balance before struck through and the balance after beside it, never "left".
- **The laptop.** The band (the pill across the top of the screen), the chips, the sheets, the toasts and the phone sit in a centred column of phone width; the world fills the screen around it; the fan opens at a larger radius. One layout rule, measured from the screen.
- **The desktop door.** Before the world, a wide window is used, never a phone stretched: the figure stands big on a stage with the travel data page filling in beside it, the choices sit in a panel docked at the right with the one yellow action at its foot, and the steps are tabs in a band across the top. Nothing scrolls the page, every look and every life shows at once, the action stays in one place, and the arrows, Enter and R work, with a line of the keys on a fine pointer. The phone's door is unchanged below the break point (`tuning.city.desktopMinWidth` wide and 640 px tall).

## 3. The fan

Tapping a thing in the world opens no sheet. The acts fan out of the thing itself: the thing is the hinge, a curve sweeps sideways and down from it into the larger free side of the screen, and the acts sit along the curve as round sticker buttons with a name pill outside the curve and the price pill. Up to six, a seventh peeking below the fold; a drag moves them along the curve; the buttons follow the thing as the camera eases, as the marks do. When the free space cannot hold the fan, or a thing has more than eight acts, the same acts open as a sheet of the same buttons. A list is a list: jobs, routes, the market and any worded choice (an ask, an agent) are sheets or phone apps, never a fan (world:fan-of-sticker-buttons).

## 4. The brief and the marker

A brief is a step of the journey handed over as a mission card by the person who sets it, in their voice, floating out of where they stand: their words first, the title as a wink ("Papers, abeg"), a picture of what you are after, the three things as sticker tiles with the two-word place under each, one look for each state (a thing done recedes with a tick and "Done" in its place's stead, the thing now filled and ringed, the rest plain; a thing met before the brief stands first, saying where it happened), the reward as picture chips, the next brief faint, one button "Let's go". At the place, the step's person or thing carries a marker: a white pill with two words and an arrow, a soft bounce, a yellow ring at the person's feet. A thing done ticks with a pop and a toast in the giver's voice; the close brings the card back with the stamp, confetti and the next briefs as picture cards. Nothing of this touches the fun: the beer joint, the barber, the suya man and the club get no brief, marker or toast, ever. **Only in the moment**: nothing sits under the band by default; the chip appears for about two seconds after a brief is handed, then slides into the Phone thumb button, whose Japa icon takes a dot, and again while you are at the step's place or beside its person; walk out and it goes (world:nudge-says-one-thing). The chip says which brief, the marker says what to do. The Japa app is the log and the map's strip carries the step's place; nothing in the world is a checklist.

## 5. The talk budget

Two channels and nothing more. Pictures over people (a plate, a naira sign, a danfo, a face) carry the ambient life and cost no reading: at most two residents at a time, turning every few seconds. Words over a person come only when that person is near the player's figure (about 7 m): at most one such bubble on screen, at most eight words, about four seconds, then thirty seconds of quiet between overheard lines; the bottom corner is for lines about the player alone. A long line shows its words clipped to two lines, and a tap opens it whole in a sheet with the speaker's name. At most three callouts over the scene at once (the nudge's chip, the one line under the band that says the next thing to notice; a word bubble; the marker; an open fan); nothing speaks over a sheet, the phone or the map, nor in the first three seconds in a new place; a crowd raises the pictures, never the words. The place's overheard lines sort into the two channels by length (world:street-talk-budget).

## 6. What the look covers

The look dresses the band, the nudge, the act surfaces, the ask, the ride sheet, the results, every phone app and the sheet frame. Its own parts are the fan, the picture tiles, the mission card and marker, the talk budget, the night paint, the centred column, toasts that answer every tap, and the market and buy mode, the mode in which the things in your room are lifted and moved (world:room-things-and-buy-mode).

Drawn on their own terms: the two lights over the start screen (account:two-lights-and-wink-lines), the door, the data page and its stamps, the prologue, the world and its things, the sky and the street lamps, the scene's answers, the sleep, the painted map and the ride, the handset with its lock screen and apps, the slips, Papers, the moment and share cards, the interview room and the December scene.

## 7. Motion and sound

Things move the way people already like, and nothing moves for long (world:motions-under-half-second: under half a second, transform and opacity only, reduced motion cuts to a fade):

- **Press.** Every tappable thing squashes a little on press (scale 0.96) and springs back on release; the yellow action's navy underside flattens.
- **Appear.** A sticker wobbles in (a small rotation that settles, 260 ms); the fan's buttons spring out along the curve one after another, 40 ms apart; a chip pops (260 ms); a sheet rises; a toast slides in from the top and out again.
- **Answer.** A tick pops with a short overshoot; a coin flies from the money toast to the band's counter and the counter counts; a brief's close brings confetti and a stamp that lands with weight; the marker bounces softly and its ring breathes.
- **Refuse.** A locked card or a short balance shakes sideways three times each way in 280 ms, and nothing else happens.
- **Sound.** The game's own cue set (`apps/client/src/shared/sound/cues.ts`, short tones made in the browser, under the mute switch; `05-audio.md` section 2): a tap on a main button, a bright `ding` for a thing done, a `coin` for money, a `thud` for a stamp, a low double note, `no`, for a refusal, a `whoosh` for the fan opening. Every sound has a visible cause; `ding`, `thud` and `no` are each under 300 ms, and every cue ends inside half a second. Nothing needs a recording.

## 8. Alternatives considered

- **A document look.** Patterned headers, small capitals, hairline rows and stamped buttons read as Lagos paperwork, and as a form: a life sim that looks like an office. The light system keeps Lagos in the world and the words.
- **A sheet for every tap.** Opening a list of acts over the scene for each thing hides the scene the act is in; the fan keeps the thing and its acts together, and the sheet stays for a long list.
- **A theme a player picks.** A second look to keep and test on every screen; the night paint is the one variation, and the world's clock drives it.
- **Corner chatter.** Lines in the corner from nobody in particular fill the screen with reading; the talk budget keeps words to people near the player and pictures for the rest.
- **Faces as pictures of people.** A face render never matches every player's picture of a person; a name pill and a flat mark do.

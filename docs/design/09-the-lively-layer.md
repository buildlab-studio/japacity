# The lively layer

> The design of the interface's look: the game is visual and light, never serious and dense. Decision record: `../decisions/0025-the-lively-layer.md`. The gameplay and every structure stay (page 08); this page is the layer on top.
> The game carries the layer: the system and the night paint (`apps/client/src/index.css`), the fan and the brief with its marker (`apps/client/src/features/city/fan`, `brief`), the talk budget, the cues of section 7 (`apps/client/src/shared/sound/cues.ts`), and the market and buy mode (decision 0019).

## 1. The decision in one line

One light, friendly system over the world: pale sky surfaces, white cards, navy ink, danfo yellow for the one action; a picture on every choice, cut as a sticker from our own models; titles of a few words in Rubik and one line in our voice; cost, time and effect as tilted sticker chips; the acts of a thing fanned out of the thing itself in the scene; the same shapes in navy at the world's night. Lagos lives in the world and the words; the interface gets out of the way.

## 2. The system

- **Surfaces.** The sheet is pale sky (`--sheet`, a very light cool blue), cards are white with a 20 px radius, a thin navy edge and a soft shadow; the ink is navy; chips are tinted pills; the one action is danfo yellow with a 4 px navy underside that flattens on press. No sand, beige or orange anywhere; no pattern on everyday surfaces. The circle pattern and the stamps live on the travel data page only.
- **Night.** When the world's clock (clock:game-day-on-real-time) says night, the same shapes repaint: navy surfaces, cream ink and edges, the yellow action unchanged. One system, two paints, driven by what already drives the sky.
- **Type.** Rubik, self-hosted, in three sizes: 22 for titles and money, 16 for body, 12 for chips; sentence case; tabular figures for money. The Danfo display face stays on the wordmark only. No tracked capitals, handwriting, chalk or monospace outside the data page and a slip.
- **Pictures.** Every choice, thing, place and reward carries a picture: a render of the game's own model where one exists (`tools/assets/shoot.ts`, the `tile` view), cut as a sticker with a white edge and a slight tilt; an emoji stands in where no model exists, and `apps/client/src/shared/tiles/missing.ts` lists those stand-ins. People are shown by a name pill with a flat mark, never by a face render.
- **Tints by kind.** A round act button and a tile take a soft tint by what the act is: food peach, study blue, money green, body lilac, people rose; everything else stays the sheet's tint.
- **Words.** A title of at most four words, one line under it in the speaker's voice (`../rules/text.md`), never the rule's words; cost, time and effect as chips ("₦1,500", "A morning", "+Belly"); a locked act says why in three words.
- **Chips earn their place**: a chip says only what is not obvious. A price shows only where there is one; "Free" shows only on an act a player would expect to pay for (a meal, a ride), never on sleep, a bath or water, and never beside a "No cost" that says the same. The time shows only when it is more than a morning (a day, a weekend, a week), never "a morning" on every card. An effect chip is at most two per card and never repeats the one line. The fan carries the price pill alone: the balance before struck through and the balance after belong to a sheet where there is room (the ride sheet, a purchase), not to a round button in the scene; the band's money counter is the balance, and while a fan button is pressed the counter shows what would be left, then settles back if the press is released. No colour key on screen: the tints speak for themselves or they are not needed.
- **One action.** One main button a surface, a verb and the thing ("Apply · Junior developer", "Let's go"). Where every card is a tap (the fan, the market), there is no button: the card is the tap and the price is a yellow pill on it, with the balance before struck through and the balance after beside it, never "left".
- **The laptop.** The band, the chip, the sheets, the toasts and the phone sit in a centred column of phone width; the world fills the screen around it; the fan opens at a larger radius. One layout rule, measured from the screen.

## 3. The fan

Tapping a thing in the world opens no sheet. The acts fan out of the thing itself: the thing is the hinge, a curve sweeps sideways and down from it into the larger free side of the screen, and the acts sit along the curve as round sticker buttons with a name pill outside the curve, the price pill and the struck balance beside it. Up to six, a seventh peeking below the fold; a drag moves them along the curve; the buttons follow the thing as the camera eases, as the marks do. When the free space cannot hold the fan, or a thing's acts are a long list, the same acts open as a sheet of sticker cards. A list is a list: jobs, routes, the market and any worded choice (an ask, an agent) are sheets or phone apps, never a fan. The fan is also buy mode's hand in the room (Move, Turn, Sell round a thing you own).

## 4. The brief and the marker

A journey step is a mission card handed over by the person who sets it, in their voice, floating out of where they stand: their words first, the title as a wink ("Papers, abeg"), a picture of what you are after, the three things as sticker tiles with ticks and the two-word place under each, the reward as picture chips, the next brief faint, one button "Let's go". At the place, the step's person or thing carries a marker: a white pill with two words and an arrow, a soft bounce, a yellow ring at the person's feet. A thing done ticks with a pop and a toast in the giver's voice; the close brings the card back with the stamp and the next briefs as picture cards. Nothing of this touches the fun: the joint, the barber, the suya man and the club get no brief, marker or toast, ever. **Only in the moment**: nothing sits under the band by default; the chip appears for about two seconds after a brief is handed, then slides into the phone whose Japa icon takes a dot, and again while you are at the step's place or beside its person; walk out and it goes. The chip says which brief, the marker says what to do. The Japa app is the log and the map's strip carries the step's place; nothing in the world is a checklist.

## 5. The talk budget

Two channels and nothing more. Pictures over people (a plate, a naira sign, a danfo, a face) carry the ambient life and cost no reading. Words over a person come only when that person is near the player's figure, at most one such bubble on screen, at most eight words, about four seconds, then at least twenty of quiet; the bottom corner is for lines about the player alone. A line longer than eight words never floats: the person shows "…" and a tap opens it in a sheet with their name. At most three callouts over the scene at once (the nudge chip, a word bubble, the marker, an open fan count one each); nothing speaks over a sheet, the phone or the map, nor in the first seconds in a new place; a crowd raises the emotes, never the words. The place's overheard lines sort into the two channels by length. The player's own figure has four emotes, wave, clap, sit and dance, and no other: dance plays one of the place's own loops and the player never controls a move, and an emote is shown only on the player's own screen.

## 6. What the layer covers

The layer leaves as they are: the two lights, the door, the data page and its stamps, the prologue, the world and its things, the sky and the lamps, the scene's answers, the sleep, the painted map and the ride, the handset with its lock screen and apps, the slips, Papers, the moment and share cards, the interview room and the December scene. It dresses the band, the nudge, the act surfaces, the ask, the ride sheet, the results, every phone app and the sheet frame. Its own parts are the fan, the picture tiles, the mission card and marker, the talk budget, the night paint, the centred column, toasts that answer every tap, and the market and buy mode. It leaves out corner chatter tied to nobody, a document look (patterned headers, small capitals, hairline rows, mustard buttons with black edges, the rule's words on screen), the month's close as a statement, a second theme and comparison surfaces, a classic page, and a fly-over fallback.

## 7. Motion and sound

Things move the way people already like, and nothing moves for long (world:motions-under-half-second: under half a second, transform and opacity only, reduced motion cuts to a fade):

- **Press.** Every tappable thing squashes a little on press (scale 0.96) and springs back on release; the yellow action's navy underside flattens.
- **Appear.** A sticker wobbles in (a small rotation that settles, about 250 ms); the fan's buttons spring out along the curve one after another, 40 ms apart; a chip pops; a sheet rises; a toast slides in from the top and out again.
- **Answer.** A tick pops with a short overshoot; money flies from the act to the band's counter and the counter counts; a reward chip flies to where it lands; a brief's close brings confetti and a stamp that lands with weight; the marker bounces softly and its ring breathes.
- **Refuse.** A locked card or a short balance shakes sideways three times, short and quick, and nothing else happens.
- **Sound.** The game's own cue set (`apps/client/src/shared/sound/cues.ts`, short synthesised tones under the mute switch) includes a soft tick on press, a bright ding for a thing done, a coin for money in, a thud for a stamp, a low double note for a refusal, a whoosh for the fan opening. Every sound has a visible cause, none lasts over 300 ms, none plays over another, and the night plays them softer. Nothing that needs a recording.

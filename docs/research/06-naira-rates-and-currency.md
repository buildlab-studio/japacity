# Two naira rates, the bank, the street, and markets in games

> Research, 7 Oct 2026, from Nigerian and international press, central bank announcements, and game wikis.
> For game design. Not financial advice. Unverified items are listed at the end.

## 1. The two rates against the dollar

| When | Official | Street | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late 2015 | about 197 (pegged) | about 258 | 31% |
| Feb 2017 | 305 | 511 | 67%, the widest |
| Sep 2021 | 412 | 530 | 29% |
| Sep 2022 | 436 | 720 | 65% |
| 14 Jun 2023, the float | 477 falls to about 750 | about 750 | Closed for weeks |
| Dec 2023 | 845 | 1,240 | 47% |
| Feb 2024 | 1,543 | 1,900 | 23% |
| End 2025 | 1,429 | 1,470 | 3% |
| 2 Oct 2026 | 1,328 | about 1,380 | 4% |

**What opens the gap**: official dollars are rationed while demand is unmet, whole categories of goods are barred from official dollars, remittances flow to the street for a better rate, and the central bank switches supply to exchange bureaus on and off.

## 2. Getting foreign currency through a bank

- **Travel allowance** at the official rate: $4,000 a quarter in 2017, needing a passport, a ticket, and travel within two weeks. Cut to $2,000 twice a year in 2023.
- **School fees**: paid straight to the university after a form, a tax certificate, the admission letter, and the invoice. Up to $15,000 a term in 2017. Capped at $7,500 a semester in 2023, with processing going from 48 hours to a stated minimum of 16 weeks.
- **Real cases, Aug 2023**: a parent who filed in June still had no dollars two months later. Another family paid ₦5.5m for £5,000 on the street.
- **Naira cards abroad**: limits fell to $20 a month at one bank in 2022. Several banks suspended them in 2023. Restored from mid 2025, and up to $40,000 a quarter at one bank by Sep 2026.
- **Dollar accounts at home**: cash deposits were capped at $5,000 a month in 2021 and freed in 2023. About $30bn sits in them. In 2024 the central bank had to deny a rumour that it would convert them to naira.
- **Goods barred from official dollars**: 41 items from 2015, later 43.

## 3. The street

- **Hubs**: a district in Abuja, roundabouts and a market in Ikeja, the Lagos airport.
- **Spread** between buying and selling: about 1% in calm times.
- **How it goes wrong**: short counts, a distraction while notes vanish.
- **The rate website episode, 2021**: the central bank called a popular site that published street rates "illegal and criminal". The site stopped publishing.
- **Crackdowns, 2024**: arrests of changers in Abuja. A crypto exchange's peer-to-peer trading was shut and two of its executives detained.
- **Licensing**: 4,173 of 5,691 exchange bureau licences were revoked in 2024 and capital requirements raised sharply. 82 were licensed under the new rules by late 2025.
- **Round-tripping**: buying dollars at the official rate and selling on the street. In 2021 banks were told to publish the names of customers who took travel allowance with fake visas or cancelled tickets.
- **The word "aboki"** means friend in Hausa. Whether it is derogatory as a label for northern traders was publicly debated in 2020.

## 4. Proof of funds

- **UK study route**: funds must stay above the line for 28 days in a row, valued in pounds at a published rate. A falling naira can push a naira balance under the line mid-window.
- **Canada**: a 2025 court ruling upheld a refusal over unexplained large deposits. Officers check the source and want six months of statements.
- **Mid-application falls**: in July 2023 the naira cost of the same dollar amount went from about ₦471,000 to ₦800,000. Students described paying almost double.

## 5. Money coming home

- **The payout rule keeps flipping**: recipient's choice of dollars (2020), a bonus per dollar received (2021), naira only at the official rate (2024), naira only again from May 2026.
- **Most remittances were said to bypass official channels** when the gap was wide, because the street paid more.
- **Fees**: about 8 to 9% to send $200 through traditional channels. Diaspora apps earn from a fee plus the rate.

## 6. Dollars as a hedge

- $10,000 was worth ₦4.7m just before the float and ₦15.4m eight months later.
- Nigeria takes about 60% of sub-Saharan stablecoin inflows. Hedging is one reason given.

## 7. Markets in games

| Game | How the market works | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| An island life-sim's turnips | Buy on Sunday, sell by Saturday. Price changes twice a day in one of four weekly patterns | The strongest daily-return hook found. Players shared prices in huge numbers and visited each other. Also became a chore, and the price code was extracted and solved |
| An open-world crime game | Two stock exchanges. Missions move prices predictably | An attempt by players to rig one failed because it tracked in-world events |
| A browser crime game | Prices update every minute and follow real stocks | Real data can be used |
| An online role-playing game | An exchange with guide prices. Trade limits to stop real-money trading caused a riot and were removed after a 91% vote | Limits anger players unless the reason is plain |
| A space trading online game | Staff economists publish monthly reports on prices and money supply | Inflation has to be managed with sinks |
| A survival shooter | Three currencies with slow-drifting ratios and a spread | Multiple currencies work when each buys different things |
| An old street-trading game and its ancestors | Prices differ by place, random spikes, a loan shark | Place-to-place differences create stories |

**Real data or simulated?** Both exist. The sensitivity is specific to this subject: the central bank has called publishing street rates illegal, and has blamed peer-to-peer platforms for the naira's weakness.

## 8. What this means for design

1. **Two rates.** The official one is slow and rationed. The street one is noisy and instant. The gap comes from a "supply of official dollars" variable and ranges from about 3% in calm to about 65% in crisis.
2. **The bank is cheap but gated**: quotas, queues that stretch to months in a crisis, documents. The street is instant with a premium and a small risk.
3. **Money is either documented or cash.** Applications need documented money held for 28 days with deposits explained. Street cash does not qualify until it has a history.
4. **Rate moves can break an application in progress.**
5. **Dollars are a hedge with policy risk.** Rules on dollar accounts and payouts change.
6. **Remittances are a choice of channel**: fee plus rate, with rule flips.
7. **Round-tripping is a tempting hustle** whose enforcement rises with the gap.
8. **Tick the rate twice a real day** on a weekly pattern players can learn. This is the daily reason to open the game.
9. **Cap what speculation can earn**, so trading currency cannot replace the journey.
10. **Simulate, calibrated to history.** Do not show a live feed of real street rates. Use the real past only in dated scenarios.
11. **Choose the in-game name for street changers with care.**

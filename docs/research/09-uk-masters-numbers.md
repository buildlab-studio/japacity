# UK master's route and money at home: working numbers

> Research, 7 Oct 2026, from official pages and press. For game design. Not immigration advice. Every figure carries its date.

## A1. Fees paid to the UK government

| Figure | Value | In force since | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student visa fee, applying from outside the UK | £558 (was £524) | 8 Apr 2026 | gov.uk; UKCISA; Queen's University Belfast |
| Immigration health surcharge, students | £776 a year | 6 Feb 2024 | gov.uk; UKCISA |
| Surcharge for a 12-month course | £1,164 (1.5 years) | 6 Feb 2024 | gov.uk, calculated from its rules |
| Priority decision | £500 extra, usually 5 working days | Not stated on page | gov.uk |
| Super priority decision | £1,000 extra, usually next working day | Not stated on page | gov.uk |
| Graduate route fee | £937 (was £880) | 8 Apr 2026 | UKCISA |

- **How the surcharge is rounded.** The surcharge is charged on the whole visa, not the course. Part of a year of 6 months or less costs half a year. More than 6 months costs a full year.
- **Why a 12-month course costs 1.5 years.** A course of 12 months or more gets 1 month before the start and 4 months after the end (Appendix Student ST 25.3), so 17 months. That is 1 year plus 5 months, and 5 months is charged as half a year.
- **An 11-month course costs the same.** It gets 1 month before and 2 months after, so 14 months, which is also charged as 1.5 years.
- **Priority** is offered only where the visa centre has it. You find out when you apply.

## A3. The 28-day rule and the exchange rate

| Rule | Exact words or summary | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Holding period | "You must have this money for at least 28 days in a row." | gov.uk, Student visa: money |
| How recent | "The end date of the 28-day period must be within 31 days of the date you apply for your visa." | gov.uk, Student visa: money |
| Statement date | "The most recently dated piece of financial evidence must be dated within 31 days before the date of application." | Appendix Finance FIN 7.1 |
| Counting back | Holding periods are counted "back from the date of the closing balance on the most recently dated piece of financial evidence." | FIN 7.2 |
| Exchange rate | Foreign currency is converted to pounds "using the spot exchange rate which appears on www.oanda.com for the date of the application." | FIN 1.1 |
| What does not count | Overdrafts. Shares, bonds, credit cards and pensions that cannot be withdrawn at once. Banks that are not regulated or cannot be verified | FIN 2.1, 3.1, 8.2 |

- The balance must not dip below the required total on any day of the 28.
- The rate is taken on the day of application, not the day the money was deposited. A naira fall during the 28 days can leave a player short.
- The 28-day rule does not apply to money from an approved student loan or official sponsor (ST 12.6).

## A4. Whose money counts

| Source of money | Counts? | Proof | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applicant's own account, alone or joint | Yes | Statement; applicant must control the funds | FIN 5.1, 5.2 |
| Parent's or legal guardian's account | Yes | "Proof of that relationship and written consent from the parent or legal guardian to use those funds" | FIN 5.3 |
| Partner's account | Only where the partner is also an applicant or holds permission as the partner | Relationship evidence | FIN 5.1 (summary) |
| Student loan | Yes, if from a government, a government-sponsored student loan company, or an academic or educational loan scheme run by a lender regulated for student loans in its country | Lender's letter dated within 6 months of applying, no conditions on release except a visa grant | FIN 8.3(c), 9.2 |
| Official financial sponsor | Yes: UK government, the applicant's own government, the British Council, or an international organisation, international company, university or independent school | Sponsor letter or amount on the CAS | FIN 8.3(b), 9.1 |
| Uncles, siblings, friends | Not listed for adult students | Not applicable | FIN 5.1 |

- A taught master's student cannot bring a partner (see A12), so in practice a partner's money counts only through a joint account.
- The rules do not name ordinary personal loans. Money borrowed elsewhere and paid into the account is simply the applicant's balance on paper, and its source becomes a credibility question (see A11 and B18).

- **Interviews.** Universities say the Home Office aims to interview most student applicants. Abroad, it is usually a video call at the visa centre, in English. Missing it means refusal (University of the West of Scotland guide, Jan 2026).
- **Topics asked**: why the UK and not elsewhere, why this university and course, modules and assessment, how fees and living costs are funded and any loans repaid, career plans, past study and gaps, the 20-hour work limit.
- **Stated refusal causes** (press and universities): answers that do not show a genuine student, document errors, money that cannot be verified, false documents. False documents can bring a ban of up to 10 years (Home Office, quoted by The PIE News, 2022).
- The Home Office disputes that refusals rose across the board (Sep 2026).

## B18. "Proof of funds" services

- **How it is reported to work**: an agent or lender places money in the student's account, or a parent's, for the holding period. The money is returned after the visa decision. Ads offered funds for 30 to 180 days (The PIE News, Mar 2023). The same money may be recycled for several students (Times Higher Education, Feb 2022).
- **Reported cost**: 5% a month from microfinance banks (Nairametrics, 2021 and 2022). No flat agent fee or percentage was found in press.
- **Reported risks**: refusal when deposits are sudden or unexplained, ten-year bans for false documents, students arriving without money for rent or fees, and lost tuition deposits after credibility refusals.
- A former UK universities minister called the practice "commonplace" (Feb 2022).

## B19. How families fund it

- **Land sales**: BusinessDay (Mar 2025) reports that almost everyone who leaves has sold property, mostly land, and that relatives abroad then buy land back. Anecdotal, no survey.
- **Saving**: "japa" was the third most common saving goal among PiggyVest users, and the top goal for over half of its Gen Z users (survey of over 1,000, Apr to May 2023).
- **Sources in a 2022 survey** of over 4,000 people: 43% of UK-minded respondents planned to seek scholarships. 8% planned to fund themselves (Vive Africa, via The PIE News).
- **Whole-family saving**: families save up to $50,000 per child (StudySearch, via Quartz, 2015).
- **Thrift (ajo, esusu) and cooperatives**: no reporting found that quantifies them for study abroad.

## What this means for the game's numbers

1. **Proof total, cheap route.** Teesside £17,000 minus a £4,000 deposit, plus £10,827 living money (from 30 Nov 2026) is **£23,827**. At ₦1,758 to the pound that is about **₦41.9m** held for 28 days.
2. **Proof total, middle route.** Leicester £24,250 minus a £6,000 deposit, plus £10,827, is **£29,077**, about **₦51.1m**.
3. **Cash out before the visa** (cheap route): deposit £4,000, visa £558, surcharge £1,164, so **£5,722** (about ₦10.1m). Add ₦120,000 TB test, about ₦285,500 IELTS and ₦100,000 passport.
4. **Real cost of the year** (cheap route): fees £17,000, visa and surcharge £1,722, and 12 months of living at the visa rate, £14,436. About **£33,000**, or about ₦58m. A £2,000 to £6,000 automatic discount takes a real bite out of it.
5. **Months of pay to hold ₦41.9m**: about 600 at minimum wage, 280 to 600 for a customer-service agent, 105 to 170 for a junior developer, 77 for a top bank trainee, 21 to 60 for a mid-level developer, and 6 to 13 for a remote developer paid in dollars. That is gross pay with nothing spent.
6. **Borrowing the proof costs real money.** At the reported 5% a month, holding ₦41.9m for about two months costs about ₦4.2m in interest. It also raises the refusal risk.
7. **Refusal is a live risk**: model about 20% for Nigerians in 2026, against 4% in early 2025. A credibility refusal can cost the deposit.
8. **Clocks**: CAS is valid 6 months, TB certificate 6 months, IELTS 2 years, bank evidence 31 days, decision 3 weeks. From 1 Jan 2027 the Graduate route shrinks to 18 months.
